# INITIATION NIGHT — the cabin ceremony

The rewrite of Initiation Night. The line-up and wrong-answer execution stay in
the clearing. Palace evidence then clears Willy, confirms Sauce as the rat with
inside help, and triggers the nuclear option: all five remaining prospects
kneel at once. Prospect Three, Four, Five, and Kittenboss are executed in
Tony's view. Lou stops Tony's execution, and Tony alone is walked through the
woods to an old country cabin and made.

## Why the cabin

The owner, verbatim:

> *"A woods initiation in an old country cabin feels more primal, secretive, and
> old-school than doing it in a mansion room. More buried family tradition, less
> conference room with cigarettes. Real if-you-know-you-know energy."*

Less like a meeting. More like induction into an ancient backwoods crime order.
Everything in the staging serves that: timber beams, a wood stove, a candle, a
blade on a table, and sixteen men standing in one long timbered room with their
coats still on.

## THE RULES OF THIS SCENE

These are not style notes. Break one and the scene stops working.

**1. The family states only the facts that cause the nuclear option.** Willy was
not the rat. Sauce was, Palace evidence proves he had inside help, and there is
one place because the family is at quota. Nobody identifies the helper.

**2. Gratin and Seff are gentle, well-liked men, and they play it completely
straight.** They are unhurried, polite, matter-of-fact. They give
**instructions, not threats.** They are never cruel and they never once enjoy
it. Nothing in their behaviour may acknowledge the joke of who is doing this.
That they are the two nicest men in the family — and that Gratin is also the
family's torture guy — is the joke, and the joke is **outside** the scene.

**3. They apologise only for logistics.** The mud, the wet, the wait, the cold.
Never for the thing itself. This is the single hardest line to hold and it is
the whole register.

**4. Kittenboss is "she", everywhere.** Dialogue, subtitles, comments,
character data, ledgers. When this file was written the Special Meeting still
said "he" throughout and the pronoun note below was an open item; that pass was
carried out on 2026-08-20, so `docs/SPECIAL-MEETING-SCRIPT.md`,
`src/specialmeeting/script.js`, `src/specialmeeting/cast.js`,
`src/core/characters.js`, `src/core/wardrobe.js`, `src/core/appearances.js` and
the manifest's booth note now agree with this document. **Anything new must
match them.**

**5. Kittenboss dies explicitly.** She kneels beside Tony and is the fourth
fatal sweep target. Tony sees it and remains free to look.

**6. Lou owns the interruption.** He lets all four executions happen, then
stops Gratin before Tony's shot: **"Stop. This one is good."**

**7. The clearing is never mentioned again.** Once the walk begins, the bodies
do not exist. No callback, no dialogue, no final camera pass over them. The
scene's discipline about this is what makes the last shot mean anything.

Three places, in order.

### The clearing

**There is one large bonfire and there is no stage.** A track through black woods
opens into nineteen metres by eight of churned, rutted, standing-water mud.
**Two cars sit nose-in at the south edge with their engines running and their
headlights left on**, crossing at the working ground. West of the ceremonial
aisle, the bonfire burns inside its stone ring without blocking the player's
route to the line.

The bonfire is the clearing's major visual anchor: crossed logs, active flame, smoke, rising embers, warm
flicker across the formal suits and moving shadows against the woods. That is the
difference the owner asked for: *"More buried family tradition, less conference
room with cigarettes."*

**What the light does, and it does three jobs at once:**

- **The beams come from behind the prospect line**, so the men standing in it —
  including the player — are **black shapes**, and the mud in front of them is
  **white**.
- **They cross at the kneel marks**, so a kneeling figure is lit from both sides
  and **the man behind them is not.**
- **They are low**, because a headlight is at knee height, and everything it
  throws a shadow of is enormous.

**And the third car.** A Lincoln parked arse-on to the mud with **its boot
standing open** — the one Kittenboss climbed out of an hour ago. It is the first
thing the player's eye finds when he is marched in, and it is **still standing
open, empty, behind everything that happens next.** Nobody shuts it.

**The line-up is unchanged and must stay unchanged.** `LINE_Z`, `PLAYER_SLOT`
and `PROSPECT_XS` in `site.js` are deliberate **copies** of `main.js`'s, and a
staging test asserts they still match. Prospect One at -4.4, the player's lit
slot at -2.2, Prospects Three, Four and Five at 0, 2.2 and 4.4.

**Kittenboss stands east of all of them, and she needs a slot of her own.**
`PROSPECT_XS` must not grow — that would break the copy assertion — so she wants
a separate exported constant, `KITTENBOSS_SLOT = { x: 6.6, z: LINE_Z }`, on the
same 2.2 m spacing as the rest of the row. **She is on the end because she came
out of the boot last and was put on the end.** Nobody arranged this
thoughtfully; the family is not sentimental about seating.

It also puts her **standing directly in front of the open boot she climbed out
of**, which is where she is for the whole of Act One, and which nobody ever
mentions.

**She is the only one in the line with a name.** Prospects One and Three through
Five get numbered nameplates as today. Hers reads **KITTENBOSS**. The player
knows exactly one person in this row, and it does not help.

### The working ground

Four kneel marks, and **they walk westward, toward the player** — seven metres
away, then five and a half, then four, then under three. **The escalation is the
staging doing the work no line of dialogue is allowed to do.** By the time the
last one is walked out, she is being put down close enough for him to reach.

| Order | Mark | Victim | Distance from the player's slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `kneel-1` | PROSPECT THREE | ~7 m |
| 2 | `kneel-2` | PROSPECT FOUR | ~5.5 m |
| 3 | `kneel-3` | PROSPECT FIVE | ~4 m |
| 4 | `kneel-4` | **KITTENBOSS** | **under 3 m** |

They are put down **facing the line** — square-ish to it, each turned a little in
toward its centre and nudged off square, because they are being pushed down by
hand and not parked. **The player is in the line, so facing the line is facing
him. He gets their faces, which is the entire point of doing it in front of
him.**

The executioner ends up **north of the mark — further from the player than the
man he is shooting** — so he can never stand in front of what the player is
meant to be watching. He stands at the length of his own forearm and reaches,
and he steps a hand's width off the spine, which is also what puts the muzzle
flash clear of the skull from where the player is standing.

**Prospect One is not on a mark.** He steps one pace forward out of the line to
answer, gets it wrong, and is shot **standing**, which is what `main.js` already
does. His body topples **backward**, so it lands between his mark and the line,
and all four kneel marks are east of it with metres to spare.

### The trail

**Thirty-six metres**, unlit, one man wide, and it **bends twice** — so the porch
light does not appear until the second bend, and **once it does, the clearing
behind is out of sight.** Nothing on it is signed and nothing on it is
explained. The player works out where he is going when the light shows up
through the trunks.

### The cabin

**Old, private, important — and not rundown.** Twelve metres by eight and a half
of squared timber with a stone chimney: a big building for the woods and a small
one for a family that owns this much of them.

Deliberately **not picturesque**. No porch swing, no lanterns strung up, no sign.
**One light over the door**, smoke going straight up, and two cars already in the
yard that were here before anybody arrived. A working porch, two metres deep, the
kind boots come off on.

**Two structural requirements that come out of that table:**

**1. The blank objective is only legal while something else is moving.** During
Acts Two, Four and Five the objective bar is empty because there is genuinely
nothing for the player to do, and that is the scene's existing convention for a
watch-this beat. It is only safe because **every one of those beats has a
timeout.** An empty objective on a beat that can stall is the armory bug
verbatim.

**2. Retire the bare-string phase machine.** `phase` is currently a plain
string advanced by `setPhase(next)` and read by **three hand-synchronised
if/else chains** — `updatePhase`, `updateCamera` and the member-facing block.
This rewrite roughly doubles the state count. Before adding a single new phase,
replace it with a frozen table:

```
PHASES = { <id>: { objective, enter(), advance(dt), timeout, next, camera } }
```

**The specific trap:** `updateCamera`'s chain ends in a bare `else` that means
"complete", so **every new phase name anybody forgets to add silently gets the
slow victory orbit** — which, on the frame Kittenboss is shot, would be a
catastrophe nobody would find until playtest. A table with a `camera` field per
phase makes that impossible, and a test that asserts every id in the table is
reachable and has a `next` (or is terminal) closes lesson 7 properly.

# PRODUCTION APPENDIX — the nine lessons, applied to this scene

These are real defects found and fixed in this codebase in the last day. Each
one has a place in this scene where it will bite.

## 1. FACING — assert it, do not eyeball it

`Npc.faceToward(x, z, snap)` stores a `targetYaw` that `update()` chases **every
frame.** A snap now clears the target (`src/bing/cast.js`), but the lesson
stands: **in a scene where men are put on their knees and shot from behind,
verify facing every time.**

**Every number is already derived in `src/initiation/cabin/site.js`. Do not
retype one.** `KNEEL_MARKS` gives, per mark: the victim's `heading`, the
`shooter` stance and heading, the `escort` stance, the `muzzle` point and the
`head` it is behind. The shooter's side of the spine is **measured** there — both
sides are tried and the one that puts the muzzle further off the player's line
of sight wins — because hard-coding a side is how the last execution of the
night ends up happening behind the victim's own skull.

**What the ceremony code owes on top of that:** it must actually *put* people on
those headings, and it must keep them there.

**Four assertions, in a registered test, with real world-space vectors, on the
frame of the shot — for every one of the four marks:**

1. **The executioner is behind the kneeling figure.**
   `dot(facingOf(mark.heading), normalize(shooter − mark)) < -0.85`
   He is at `SHOOTER_REACH` behind and `SHOOTER_OFFSET` to one side; the dot is
   ≈ -1.
2. **The kneeling figure is turned toward the line, and therefore toward the
   player.** `dot(facingOf(mark.heading), normalize(LINE_CENTER − mark)) > 0.85`
3. **The player can see the muzzle clear of the head.** `lateralOffsetFromLine(
   PLAYER_EYE, mark.head, mark.muzzle)` must exceed `SHOULDER_HALF_WIDTH` — the
   flash is only visible at all because the shooter stepped off the spine.
4. **Nothing already on the ground blocks it.** The bodies accumulate westward
   as the marks do; assert the sightline from `PLAYER_EYE` to each mark's head
   is clear of every *previous* mark's fallen body. This is the check the
   staggered z of the four marks exists to pass, and it is the one that silently
   stops passing the moment anybody adjusts a mark by 20 cm.

**Then assert it again sixty frames later.** `Npc.faceToward(x, z, snap)` stores
a `targetYaw` that `update()` chases **every frame.** A snap now clears the
target (`src/bing/cast.js`), but this is the scene that would expose a
regression: **a man who slowly rotates back to face the cars while kneeling.**
Step the sim after the snap and re-assert #2.

**And the executioners themselves:** Gratin and Seff walk from the Circle behind
the line out to the working ground, **past the line and past the bodies already
down**. Give them an explicit path with `ESCORT_CLEARANCE`, snap their facing at
each stop, and assert the escort's waiting stance is out of the player's
sightline — `site.js` derives `escort` per mark for exactly this.

## 2. HAND SOCKETS — `parts.handL` / `parts.handR`, never a forearm

Attaching to a forearm with a magic offset is what put beer cans on golfers'
forearms. Every prop in this scene goes in a **hand**:

| Prop | Beat | Socket |
|---|---|---|
| The revolver | IN-060 → IN-170 | `handR` of the active shooter: Seff for Prospect One's barrage, Gratin for the four-shot sweep and Tony's non-firing aim |
| The blade | IN-400 | `handR` of Rippinflow → **`handR` of Lou** |
| The saint card | IN-400 → IN-440 | `handR` of Booskibro → the **player's palm** → back to Lou's `handR` on a drop |
| The candle | IN-440 | `handR` of Lou, taken off the table and put back |
| The bandana | IN-465 | `handR`/`handL` of Lou, off the table |
| Whiskey glasses | IN-500 → IN-540 | `handR` of **everybody** in the room, including the player's burned one |

The shared character mount owns the revolver's hand socket and rotation. Any
handoff from Seff to Gratin must move that one mounted prop; IN-140 no longer
exists and must not be recreated as a reload phase.

## 3. SEATING — the right base height, or he sinks through the furniture

**`STOOL_SIT = 0.315` is for stools.** A chair is a different number. Getting
this wrong sank two characters through the furniture at the Bing.

**Lou sits, at the head of the cabin table, from IN-300 to IN-370.** He is the
only person in the room who is sitting, so he is the only place this can go
wrong — and he is in the middle of the frame with the camera on him for four
straight minutes. Use the **chair** base height, and verify it in the geometry
gate rather than by eye.

Everyone else in the cabin **stands**, deliberately: sixteen people, a room built
for six, coats on. That is both the staging the owner asked for and the cheapest
possible way to avoid this bug sixteen times.

## 4. SPATIAL AUDIO — `follow`, and a gentler rolloff for dialogue

`audio.play()` takes a **`follow`** option — an `Object3D`, a vector, or a getter
— that keeps a playing sound glued to a moving speaker. **A one-shot without it
freezes at the speaker's position at the first syllable.**

**Every line spoken by anyone who is walking needs `follow`.** In this scene
that is a lot of them:

- **IN-100 → IN-170**: the speakers are stationary or making short blocking
  moves, but every line still follows its speaker. Never pin a line to the
  clearing centre.
- **The whole of ACT THREE.** Everyone is walking, for minutes. Seff's "Colder
  than it was", Lag's "It's the wet", Irish and Hog Mama at the front of the
  line, Shubes behind the player's shoulder, Numbskull's "Watch your feet through
  here" — **every single one needs `follow`** or the trail turns into a corridor
  of invisible speakers hanging in the dark where somebody used to be.
- **IN-540**: the room noise during a continuous camera pull-back **out of a
  building and into the trees**. The falloff over that move is the shot.

**`rolloff: 0.7` for dialogue**, against the 1.4 default. **The mud alone is 19 m
wide and the trail is 36 m long**; a line at 1.4 goes unintelligible long before
it reaches the player, and Gratin says most of what he says from seven metres
out with two engines idling behind the player's head. Reserve 1.4 for the world.

## 5. WEAPON AUDIO — `playWeaponCue`, never a raw cue

**Never call `audio.play` with a raw gun cue.** Go through
`playWeaponCue(audio, id, slot, opts)` in `src/core/weapons/audio.js`, which
applies the per-weapon mix from the catalog and sensible positional defaults.

**A revolver fired outdoors at night behind a kneeling man is the loudest thing
in this scene and every shot must be positional**, at the muzzle, with
`follow` on the shooter.

| Beat | Call |
|---|---|
| IN-070 | `playWeaponCue(audio, 'revolver', 'fire', { follow: seff.parts.handR })` for each intentional barrage round |
| IN-120, IN-130, IN-145 | `playWeaponCue(audio, 'revolver', 'fire', { follow: gratin.parts.handR })` |
| IN-160 | **No fire call.** Lou's IN-170 interruption wins first |
| FAIL-B | `'revolver', 'fire'` — **positional, behind the player**, on the frame the screen goes black |

Use the shared `revolver` event. `weapon.revolver.fire` is already delivered
with the heavier mix; do not mint a scene-local replacement.

**FAIL-B's shot is the hardest mix in the game**: it is behind the player's head,
indoors, in a small timber room, and the screen goes black **before the report
finishes.** Do not truncate the tail with the cut — let it ring into the black.

## 6. GEOMETRY GAPS — run the gate

Nothing may float, nothing may be buried in a wall, and no character may
intersect furniture. **This rewrite adds an entire interior**, which is where
this normally goes wrong.

**The clearing — five bodies accumulate on the ground and they must not land on
each other:**

- **The row runs east-west and the fall runs north-south, at right angles, on
  purpose.** `site.js` records why: a first pass turned each victim to face the
  player's own slot, and the far mark's body then fell **west, straight along the
  row and onto the next mark** — the second man would have been put on his knees
  on top of the first one. **Bodies fall across the working ground or they fall
  onto it, and there is no third option.** Do not re-aim a kneel heading without
  re-running the fall.
- **A man shot in the back of the head from behind falls FORWARD**, and since
  these four are turned toward the line, forward is **south, toward the player**.
  All four fall in parallel. The existing fall animation is a **backward** topple
  about the feet (`rotation.x` negative) — **correct for the standing frontal
  shot at IN-070, and wrong for all four kneeling ones.** A forward variant is
  needed: pitch forward out of the kneel, ending face-down.
  **Reusing the backward topple here is the single most likely mistake in the
  implementation, and it will read instantly as wrong.**
- **Prospect One is the exception and stays backward.** He is shot standing on
  `STAND_MARK`, he topples back toward the line, and every kneel mark is east of
  him with metres to spare.
- Assert every settled body is clear of every kneel mark, of `STAND_MARK`, and of
  the line at z = -8.
- The mud decal sits at `MUD.y` = 2 cm, **under the gate's 4 cm float gap**, and
  stops at z = -1.8 because the bonfire ring's stones start at -2.6 — a mud
  sheet drawn through a boulder is the kind of thing nobody notices until the
  screenshot.
- The three cars, the bonfire's stone ring and logs, and the **open boot lid** are
  all props on this ground and all of them are on the gate. Flame, smoke and
  embers are effects, not load-bearing geometry.

**The cabin** — everything new, everything on the gate:
- The blade, the saint card, the candle, the bottle, the glasses and the folded
  bandana **rest on the table surface**, not at table height in the air.
- Sixteen standing bodies in one room, **none intersecting each other, the
  table, the stove or the walls.** Sixteen is a lot; block it out before dressing
  it.
- The stove against the back wall, not in it. The stone chimney through the roof,
  not floating over it. Firewood on the porch resting on the boards.
- **Beams low enough to read as low, and Numbskull and DeathMegatron duck under
  them on purpose** — they are the tallest bodies in the game and they are both
  in this room. That is a staged duck, not a clip. Assert no head intersects a
  beam at any point on their paths.
- **The porch deck is 12 cm**, and that number is a movement decision, not an
  architectural one: this scene's player is a flat-ground walker with circle
  colliders and no step-up, so a real 45 cm porch would be an invisible wall
  across the only door in the level. Do not "correct" it upward.

**If the geometry gate's record-count golden moves, verify the FINDINGS are
unchanged before touching the number, and say so in the commit.** A moved count
with unchanged findings is bookkeeping; a moved count with new findings is a
regression wearing bookkeeping's coat.

`src/initiation/main.js` is currently **waived** by the gate because it is a
top-level module with no exported builder — it constructs the renderer,
composer, world, cast and HUD at import time. **The cabin should not inherit
that waiver.** Build the cabin interior as an exported builder so the gate can
see it, even if the rest of the file stays as it is.

IN-415, the two repeats at IN-430/435, and **the burning card at IN-440, which
is the endurance beat the gauntlet used to be** — a hold, in a closed fist, that
the player has to sit through.

**If the owner wants them kept**, the only place they can go is between IN-090
and IN-100 — trials passed, and *then* "Clear the line." The insertion point is
marked. It is not recommended.

**Retiring them means deleting `endured`, `trial_roar`, `roar_anim`,
`trial_log`, `log_broken`, `gauntlet_in`, `beatdown` and `fail_swing` from all
three if/else chains**, including `updateCamera`'s — see §7 about the bare
`else`. Their nine ceremony lines go in the `retired` ledger.

## 4. Where the player's question sits

**As specified: IN-085 comes before the executions**, and he is told he passed
before anybody else is killed.

The reasoning: it preserves "the question trial stays as it is today" completely
intact; **the relief is the setup**; and it makes the executions un-earned. The
others are not shot for wrong answers — Prospect Three was never asked a
question and says so — **they are shot because they are not him, and nobody
explains it.** Standing in a line having just been congratulated while four
people are killed is the scene.

The alternative — executions first, then his question — makes the quiz a
survival test, which is a **cleaner** scene and a **much smaller** one, because
then there is a reason.

## 5. Prospect One's shooter is now pinned

`startExecution` currently picks **the nearest un-knocked member** to the victim,
so who shoots Prospect One is emergent and varies. This script **pins it to
Seff**, so the same two men do all the killing and the player learns their faces
before Act Two. That is a small behavioural change to an existing beat and it is
called out here rather than buried: `startExecution` needs a `shooter` field.

## 6. The salud cannot go through the dialogue queue

`say()` drains **one line at a time.** Seventeen named lines plus three crowd
shouts, queued, is a **forty-second roll call** in which fifteen men take turns
being pleased. It must be a **crowd bed** (three overlapping recorded "SALUD!"
layers) with the named lines fired over the top at small random offsets, several
overlapping at any moment. **The owner's five fire first and clearly**; the rest
fill in around them and it does not matter if two collide, because that is what
a room sounds like.

Same problem, smaller, at **IN-460**: "Welcome." is **one word from two men in
unison.** Two takes, mixed to the same frame. If it queues, the beat dies.

## 7. The camera during the executions

**As specified: the player's look stays free.** He can look away and nothing
stops, nobody comments, and the scene does not make him watch. **Nobody noticing
is essential** — a character who says "look at me" is a character who has
noticed the scene is a scene.

The risk is that a player who looks at the trees for ninety seconds misses the
staging. Three mitigations are already built into the site: **the headlights
cross at the marks**, so the working ground is the only lit thing in front of
him; **the marks walk toward him**, so the last one is under three metres away;
and the pistol reports are positional and will pull a head round on their own.

**A soft cinematic lock is the alternative.** It is not recommended. Taking the
camera off a player during a killing tells him the game wants him to see it,
and this scene must never appear to want anything.
