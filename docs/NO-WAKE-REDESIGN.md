# NO WAKE — the redesign

Owner's direction, 2026-08-05. This is the spec. Where it and the current
build disagree, this wins.

> "No Wake should feel like a pressure cooker with navigation lights. Everyone
> knows what the trip is really for except Willy, and even Willy senses
> something is wrong. The humor comes only from the absurdly specific
> Counter-Strike lie that finally exposes him. Nobody delivers punchlines,
> nobody flails around, and the body does not bounce off three railings like a
> haunted pool noodle."

## The spine does not change

Arrive · board · startup procedure · pilot out · reach the inlet · go below ·
confront Willy · execute · wrap him with the Billy HotDog system · fetch the
weights from the bow · attach them · carry him to the stern · dump him · leave.

What changes is the boat, the staging, the dialogue, the pacing and the
temperature.

## The boat

A fictional 35–36 ft late-1980s express cabin cruiser, laid out like a
stretched 270 Sundancer: big cockpit and bow, a proper cabin with a wet bar,
a dinette and a V-berth. Modern boats are proportion reference only — this one
is period-correct.

Cream fiberglass with a dark burgundy or navy stripe · smoked wraparound
windshield · analog gauges · chrome throttle levers · cream vinyl with
weathered seams · teak or imitation teak · warm wood veneer below · brass
fixtures and amber cabin light · twin inboards with a deep steady rumble.

> "It should look like something Lou bought with cash through a guy who
> definitely did not file the correct paperwork."

### Stern
Broad swim platform just above the water — this is the disposal point. Swim
ladder, starboard transom gate, low stern rail, mooring cleats, a storage
hatch holding the tarp and the body bag, room for a two-person carry, and a
disposal zone at the aft edge.

### Aft cockpit
U-shaped cream seating, a small removable cocktail table, the engine hatch,
wet footprints, cupholders, ashtray, ropes, life jackets, the companionway,
and clear passage between the stairs and the platform. **Nobody gathers here
during the confrontation — it should feel strangely abandoned.**

### Helm (starboard)
Wheel, twin throttles, port and starboard ignition, battery selector, blower,
navigation-light switch, tachometers, fuel, depth, marine radio, compass,
warning lights. **Keep the existing startup gameplay; remove the comedic
failure reactions.** The player performs the procedure while everyone waits in
silence.

### Bow
Wide enough to walk. Stainless rail, sun pad, anchor hatch, forward locker,
navigation light, rope coil, searchlight, and the cast-iron ballast in the
forward locker. **Irish stays here almost the whole mission.**

## Below deck — the heart of it

The companionway drops into a warm enclosed cabin. Engines go muffled and
physical. Low ceiling. The hull creaks. Water taps the fiberglass. The room
should feel smaller once all four men are in it.

**Port — bar and galley:** wood-veneer counter, small stainless sink, mirrored
liquor cabinet, the tequila, four heavy-bottomed glasses, ice bucket, mini
fridge, amber light under the cabinet, one fixed swivel stool, a bottle shelf
with restrained physics. The bar stays visible throughout the confrontation.

**Starboard — curved dinette:** curved booth, mounted table, room for Willy at
the end, Lou at the far side, a clear firing line from the player, and **no
loose furniture that can become slapstick debris.**

**Forward:** a partly visible V-berth — rumpled bedding, closed curtain,
reading lamps, storage under the mattress, dark hatch above. Not used in the
confrontation; it is there for depth.

Also: closed head door, narrow aft sleeping compartment, a radio playing
faintly until Lou shuts it off, portholes showing dark water and occasional
shoreline lights, a dark runner under the seating, engine vibration
throughout.

## Flow

**Marina, dusk.** Isolated finger. Irish already aboard with binoculars.
Booski near the stern. Lou by the helm. Willy arrives with the player or
seconds later. **Nobody greets him warmly.**

> WILLY: "Nice night for it." *(no answer)*
> LOU: "Get her started."

Objectives: BOARD THE BOAT → COMPLETE THE STARTUP PROCEDURE. Battery, blower,
fuel check, port engine, starboard engine, nav lights, dock line, helm.
**No jokes if the player hesitates. Lou simply watches.**

**Leaving.** Through the no-wake channel. Irish goes forward and stays.
**Booski sits behind Willy so Willy cannot comfortably watch everyone at
once.** The NO WAKE sign passes to starboard, marina lights fall away, houses
thin out, water darkens, the score thins to engines and hull.

> WILLY: "Quiet tonight." BOOSKI: "Yeah."
> WILLY: "Where exactly are we headed?" LOU: "Away from the dock."

**The inlet.** Behind a wooded point or quarry wall.

> LOU: "Bring her down." *(then)* LOU: "Kill them."

The silence after the engines stop should be uncomfortable. Irish stays out.

> IRISH: "Channel's clear."
> LOU: "Let's get out of the wind."

Objective: GO BELOW DECK.

## The cabin scene

Lou at the far end of the dinette. Booski behind the bar. Willy standing
between bar and booth. The Prospect at the bottom of the stairs. Irish above,
at the bow. The player keeps camera control but cannot leave, and movement is
limited to a small staging area so the composition holds. Booski closes the
companionway; the engine noise all but vanishes.

**The tequila.** Booski glances at Lou first, pours **one** shot, slides it
across, pours for nobody else. Glass and bottle should be unnaturally loud.

**The Negev story.** Willy talks because nobody else will.

> WILLY: "You remember that Mirage match? We were down fourteen-twelve.
> Everybody was broke. I bought the Negev, went B, and held the whole site by
> myself." *(nothing)* *(he takes the shot)* "Three through apartments. One
> coming out market. Saved the whole damn round."
> LOU: "Willy."
> WILLY: "Yeah?"
> *(Lou waits long enough that the player wonders if the scene has stalled)*
> LOU: "Was there ever even a Negev on B?"

Silence. The boat creaks. **No exaggerated gasp, no double take** — his
shoulders settle and his eyes move between Lou, Booski and the Prospect.

> WILLY: "Lou…" LOU: "Answer the question."
> WILLY: "I don't remember every gun from every round."
> BOOSKI: "You were like a brother to me."
> WILLY: "Boosk, come on. You know me."
> LOU: "Yeah. We do now."

He looks at the stairs; the Prospect is in them. He sets the glass down.

> WILLY: "Can I take a seat?" BOOSKI: "Sit down."

He sits slowly, hands visible. **No confession monologue. No thirty seconds of
begging. The restraint is what makes it ugly.** He looks once at the ceiling,
knowing Irish is up there, then back at Lou.

> WILLY: "All right."

Lou draws. Booski draws. Objective: DRAW YOUR WEAPON. Movement locked, aim
free, **no countdown.** When the player fires, all three fire on the same
beat. Willy slumps against the booth and drops forward. The shot glass
vibrates, rolls, and stops against the sink. **No flying body, no spray, no
ragdoll comedy, no music.**

## The body

> LOU: "Finish it."

Reuse the Billy HotDog wrapping system with boat-specific staging: tarp beside
the body, roll him on, **swap the ragdoll for the stabilised wrapped-body
prefab**, player folds one side, Booski the other, player fastens the authored
straps, Booski closes the bag, and it becomes a two-person carry object. **No
knot simulation** — attachment points and authored animation, so the bag never
becomes a physics burrito.

> LOU: "Needs weight." BOOSKI: "Bow locker."

The open air should feel startling after the cabin. Irish does not turn round
and does not ask. He heard it.

> IRISH: "Nothing behind us."

Two cast-iron pieces or one bundled ballast prop, visibly heavy without being
a physics puzzle. Authored sockets on the bag; Booski secures it. **No knot
minigame, no repeated comedy prompts.**

> LOU: *(checks his watch)* "Move him."

Two-person synchronised carry: lift, rotate to the companionway, up the
stairs, through the cockpit, out the transom gate, set down on the platform.
**Design the boat around this traversal — make the companionway wider than a
realistic one so the animation stays clean.** Irish stays forward; Lou follows
and does not help carry.

Water laps inches below the bag. Lou looks at the shoreline and nods.
Objective: DUMP THE BODY. One strike on the water, it sinks, it is gone. **No
enormous splash, no floating marker, no joke.** Hold on the water for several
seconds. Nothing comes back up.

> IRISH: "Still clear." LOU: "Start her."

## Exit

LEAVE THE INLET: both engines, wait for Irish to get the anchor, into gear,
back toward open water. **Nobody speaks.** At speed, the camera looks briefly
astern at the wake spreading and smoothing over.

MISSION COMPLETE: NO WAKE

## Tone rules — non-negotiable

No comedic ragdoll · nobody trips over the body · no jokes after the execution
· no goofy weightlifting · Willy does not run around the cabin while everyone
shoots · no arcade combat music · no giant objective arrow over Willy · no
excessive blood · no sentimental speech from Lou · no twenty-line confession ·
nobody celebrates · **Irish never abandons his lookout.**

> "The Negev line is the blade entering the scene. Everything before it
> tightens the room around Willy."

Willy is not oblivious. He senses it from the dock onward and keeps talking
because silence would force him to admit it.

## The foreshadow

The Negev lands harder if it has been heard once before. Earlier in the
campaign, during the window when the family believes the leak happened, Willy
says casually:

> WILLY: "Ask anybody. I was on Mirage all night. Sat B with the Negev like an
> asshole."

Disposable Squatch chatter at the time. Lou has since checked the match. **The
player needs no technical explanation aboard the boat** — Willy understands the
question immediately, and his reaction tells the audience everything.

## Reuse, don't rebuild

Keep: marina entrance and trigger, boarding, the startup checklist, water
movement and boat controls, route markers, the weapon system, the Billy HotDog
wrapping mechanic and wrapped-body prefab, two-person carry if it exists, the
disposal trigger, the completion transition, and every existing Willy / Lou /
Booski / Irish / Prospect voice system.

Replace: the small boat model, the open-deck confrontation, the slapstick
dialogue, loose ragdoll handling, the comedy animations, the disposal staging,
and any goofy musical cue.

## Technical — a moving interior

One stable local coordinate system while aboard: parent the player, the NPCs,
the props and the local navmesh to the boat root. Simplified collision hulls
for cabin and companionway. **Anchor or kinematically lock the boat during the
confrontation.** Disable loose-object physics during the shooting. Swap the
ragdoll for the wrapped prefab before any carrying. Animation sockets for the
ballast. Synchronised root motion for the carry. Author the disposal animation
rather than simulating it. Checkpoints after the inlet, after the execution,
and after the weights.

The boat moves naturally in the marina section and goes nearly stationary for
the interior sequence — cinematic without "inviting the physics engine to
summon the poltergeist of clipping."
